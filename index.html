West Africa’s Golden Empires

Ghana, Mali and Songhai: the people, cities and trade networks that shaped a region — and the modern question of where Africa’s gold and petroleum resources are found today.

History meets geology

One region, many layers of history

The medieval Ghana, Mali and Songhai states flourished across the western Sahel. Their power was closely connected to trade — especially gold and salt — but they were not the same as the modern countries of Ghana or Mali. Today’s mineral and petroleum maps describe modern geology and exploration, not the borders or resource ownership of medieval kingdoms.

Important distinction: historical gold-trading zones do not prove that crude oil or gold lies beneath every part of an empire’s former territory. Deposits are identified through geological mapping, sampling, geophysics and drilling.

Discover the three empires

c. 6th–13th centuries

Ghana Empire (Wagadu)

Centred broadly in areas of present-day southeastern Mauritania and western Mali, Ghana grew wealthy by taxing trans-Saharan commerce. Gold from southern production zones and salt from the Sahara moved through its trading network. Its capital is commonly associated with the Kumbi Saleh area.

c. 13th–16th centuries

Mali Empire

Founded under Sundiata Keita in the 13th century, Mali expanded across much of the western Sahel. Under Mansa Musa, its wealth and links to wider Islamic and commercial networks became famous. Timbuktu, Djenné and Niani are among the important places associated with its history.

c. 15th–16th centuries

Songhai Empire

Songhai rose around Gao and reached its height under Askia Muhammad. It controlled major Niger River cities and trade routes. Agriculture in the Niger floodplain, taxation and commerce in gold and salt supported its economy. Moroccan forces defeated Songhai at Tondibi in 1591.

Dates and territorial limits are approximate; historical boundaries shifted over time and are reconstructed from written sources, oral traditions and archaeology.

Gold and crude oil in present-day Africa

Gold: established producers

West Africa contains major gold-producing belts. Ghana, Mali and Burkina Faso are important modern gold producers. Gold deposits also occur in South Africa, Tanzania, Sudan and other countries. The location and size of each deposit depend on local geology.

Crude oil: major producing regions

African petroleum production is associated with sedimentary basins, including the Niger Delta and offshore Gulf of Guinea, as well as North African basins and parts of Central and Southern Africa. Nigeria, Libya, Algeria, Angola, Egypt, Gabon and other states have petroleum industries.

Could more be discovered?

Yes. Underexplored basins and mineral belts may contain undiscovered resources. But a geological possibility is not a discovery: exploration results, commercial viability, environmental safeguards and community consent all matter.

Do not confuse the resources: gold is commonly associated with particular ancient and modern mineralised rock systems; oil and gas generally accumulate in sedimentary basins. A gold-rich region is not automatically oil-rich.
